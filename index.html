When to list

Right now it's fall, and that's generally a tough season to sell. The numbers for acreage around Sedalia back that up.

The chart below uses two years of MLS data for every property on 5 to 100 acres in your area. For each month, it shows how many homes closed compared to how many were on the market. A higher bar means buyers were absorbing more of the available inventory.

Closings as a percent of active listings, averaged by month, January 2025 through September 2026. Source: REcolorado MLS, 5 to 100 acre properties.

A few things stand out. Closings peak in May and June. Since it typically takes 30 to 60 days to get from contract to closing, those closings come from contracts written in March, April, and May. That's when acreage buyers are most active.

Inventory moves the other way. Active acreage listings are lowest in January and February, then climb about 40% by June. Fall is the weakest stretch of the year, with September through November showing the fewest closings relative to what's on the market.

If the goal is the best offer and the fastest sale, I'd recommend a listing date in late February. That puts your property on the market while competing inventory is near its low point for the year, and fully marketed right as buyer activity builds through spring. Wait until May, and you're competing with about a third more listings for the same buyers.

One honest note: even in its best months, acreage in this area carries 7 or more months of inventory. Buyers have the advantage in every season. Timing helps, but it doesn't replace pricing it right.

The late February timeline also gives us the winter to walk the property, handle any prep, line up staging, and plan photography and video so we launch ready. If your plans call for a different timeline, we can list sooner. We'd just go in knowing the season is working against us and set expectations around that.
